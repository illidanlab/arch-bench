# ARCH-Bench annotation guide

> Public edition: content-identical annotation rules; internal process references removed.
> This guide describes the complete annotation workflow including a human verification
> stage; in v1.0.0-alpha.2 that stage has not yet been performed — all labels are
> machine-proposed. Worked examples cite case ids (`ABV0-T1-####`) from an earlier corpus
> that is not published here; they are retained as internal lineage examples, because the
> rules they teach are the rules the published labels were made under. Citations to
> earlier-version documents (the v0.1 ontology, the v0.1→v0.2 mapping, and the v0.1
> annotation guide) refer to internal lineage materials not included in this repository.

Version: guide-v0.2

This guide is the shared rulebook for two readers: the LLM preannotation worker, which
proposes gold labels for one case, and the human verifier, who reviews every case and produces
the labels of record. Neither reader annotates from scratch: Stage 1 supplied the intended
truth, so the
job is to confirm which intended concerns the transcripts actually express, attach source
spans, mark omissions, add genuinely unplanned concerns, correct domain / speaker / priority /
urgency, finalize dyadic relations, and verify summary and safety labels. Preannotation is
assistance and is never verification; only a verifier-saved patch file makes a case
`human_verified`. Every rule below is meant to be executed as written — where the guide is
silent, do not invent a convention, follow "Uncertain cases" and escalate.

Domain and subdomain vocabulary lives in `ontology/ontology_v0.1.yaml`; this guide cites its
entries and does not restate them. Turn ids in examples are real turns from the
generated case records those examples cite.

**How to read guide-v0.2.** Everything before the "v0.5 flow addendum" is guide-v0.1,
carried forward unchanged — the rules, their wording, and the worked examples, none of them
renumbered or rewritten. guide-v0.1 remains unchanged at `docs/annotation_guide.md` as the
rulebook of record for the retired v0.1 corpus. What changed is the interview those rules
read: the v1 release corpus (`ABV0-R1-0001..0036`) was generated by the v0.5 script
(fixed category blocks, no follow-up questions, a six-turn numbered-menu review), so every
worked example below carries a turn-shape banner, and the
**v0.5 flow addendum** at the end supersedes the three turn-shape-dependent rules: source-span
selection, priority ranking, and the ontology file the labels come from (`ontology_v0.2.yaml`
for release cases). The judgment rules — domain assignment, speaker attribution, urgency,
the dyad maths, the summary set, safety, critical hallucination, uncertain cases, and the
adjudication workflow — are unchanged, and the examples that teach them are still the best
illustrations we have.

## Concern inclusion threshold

Apply in order to every user turn:

1. **User turns only.** `speaker: "system"` turns never source a concern, even when the system
   names the issue in a recap.
2. **Substantive and topical.** The speaker presents a problem, worry, want, or unanswered
   question about their own situation, care, or family. Pure acknowledgment
   (ABV0-T1-0001 c02 "Okay. That's fine."), meta-talk about the interview, and restatements of
   the system's words are not concerns.
3. **Recognizable without the latent truth.** A latent concern counts as expressed only if the
   speaker's own turns carry content a naive reader would recognize as that issue. If not,
   emit no gold concern and record the latent id as omitted.
4. **Hedging and minimizing do not disqualify.** ABV0-T1-0004 c12 ("I don't eat much lately,
   but that's just how it is... It's honestly the least of it") is an included concern; the
   minimization is priority evidence, not an exclusion.
5. **One concern per underlying issue.** Several turns about the same issue produce one
   concern with several spans, not several concerns. Two distinct asks inside one turn produce
   two concerns.
6. **Safety cues are not concerns.** Acute-risk statements route to the safety object (see
   "Safety categories and severity"), per the `exclude` entries of `emotional_concerns` and
   `spiritual_existential`.

## Domain assignment

1. Choose the domain first, then a subdomain from that domain's `subdomains` list. Never
   invent labels outside `ontology/ontology_v0.1.yaml`.
2. Read the candidate domain's `include` and `exclude` entries before committing. The
   `exclude` entries are the deciding text in most disputes.
3. Standard tie-breakers (all from the ontology's own `confusions` / `exclude` entries):
   - symptom vs. lost activity — `physical_symptoms` confusions: symptom in focus →
     `physical_symptoms`, lost activity in focus → `quality_of_life`;
   - unclear instruction vs. inability to execute a clear one — `treatment_questions` include
     vs. `practical_logistical` confusions;
   - a specific unanswered question vs. a pattern of being left out —
     `communication_preferences` confusions;
   - loneliness with a school/peer setting vs. without — `school_peers` confusions;
   - fairness and mortality questions are in-scope concerns, statements of intent to self-harm
     are safety events — `spiritual_existential` confusions;
   - worry about one's own feelings vs. about a family member's — `emotional_concerns`
     exclude.
4. The latent domain is a prior. Change it only when the transcript genuinely says something
   else; then set `verification_status: "modified"` and log a `domain` discrepancy.
5. If no domain fits, pick the closest, note it (see "Uncertain cases"), and flag it as an
   ontology gap for the human verifier — do not stretch a subdomain.

## Speaker attribution

1. `session` is the session the turn appears in — child turns produce child concerns, parent
   turns produce parent concerns. This holds even when the speaker is describing the other
   person. ABV0-T1-0004 c04 and c06 (the child describing the mother's exhaustion) is a
   **child** concern, `family_sibling` / `caregiver_strain`; the `emotional_concerns`
   confusions entry makes the same point for a parent describing the child's fear.
2. Never move a concern between sessions. If the transcript expressed a latent child concern
   in the parent session, keep the latent session, mark `modified`, and log a `speaker`
   discrepancy for the human verifier.
3. Attribution errors are the highest-cost label error in this benchmark: they are two of the
   six critical hallucination types. When unsure, re-check which transcript the span turn ids
   come from (`c##` = child session, `p##` = parent session).

## Source-span selection

> **v0.5 cases: read this section with the addendum.** Rule 2 below — the
> clarification turn that resolves an indirect expression — has no v0.5 analogue,
> because the v0.5 script has no clarification turns at all. The addendum's
> "Source spans under v0.5" replaces it; every other rule in this section stands.

1. `source_spans` = the turn ids of **the speaker's own user turns that express the concern**,
   listed in transcript order.
2. **Include the clarification turn that resolves an indirect expression.** An indirect opener
   plus the clarification answer is one concern with two spans.
3. Exclude system turns, including recaps that state the concern more clearly than the speaker
   did.
4. Exclude `review_prioritization` user turns unless the speaker adds content there beyond
   ranking; ranking alone is priority evidence, not expression.
5. Every active gold concern needs at least one span, and every span must be an existing turn
   id in that case (a QC check).
6. A turn may be a span for more than one concern, and a concern span may coincide with a
   safety span.

## Priority ranking

> **v0.5 cases: read this section with the addendum.** Rules 1–4 below describe the
> retired v0.1 recap review. For an `ABV0-R1-####` case the primary evidence is the
> numbered-menu selection (`r2`) and the confirmed list (`r3`) — see the addendum's
> "Priority ranking under v0.5". Rules 5 and 6 stand unchanged.

1. **Primary evidence is the `review_prioritization` exchange**: the system's recap order plus
   the speaker's answer. Emphasis elsewhere in the transcript (turn count, elaboration,
   intensity words) is secondary evidence, used only for concerns the recap did not cover or
   when the speaker's answer leaves an order underdetermined.
2. If the speaker confirms the recap ("Yes, that's right" — ABV0-T1-0014 p16,
   ABV0-T1-0036 p16), rank in recap order.
3. If the speaker reorders, the speaker wins. ABV0-T1-0004 c14 ("My mom. Definitely that one.
   The eating thing is whatever.") sets caregiver strain to 1 and eating to 3, leaving the
   hope concern at 2 from the c13 recap.
4. Concerns raised but never recapped rank after all recapped concerns, ordered by emphasis.
5. Ranks are integers 1..n, **unique within a speaker's session**, no ties and no gaps. Ranks
   in the two sessions are independent.
6. The latent priority rank is a prior only. Where the transcript orders differently, the
   transcript wins: mark `modified` and log a `priority` discrepancy.

## Urgency

Allowed values: `routine`, `elevated`, `urgent`. Urgency describes the concern **as
expressed**, not the domain, not the speaker's emotional volume, and never the safety event.

- `routine` (default): a normal item for the upcoming visit, no stated deadline and no ongoing
  interruption of care.
- `elevated`: a concrete near-term deadline, or an ongoing failure the speaker describes as
  happening now — ABV0-T1-0001 c06 ("When it's gone I just skip it") with doses actually being
  missed, or ABV0-T1-0004 p04 ("He's down again on the home scale... I'm afraid we're headed
  back to the hospital over this").
- `urgent`: the speaker asks for it to be handled at or before today's visit, or a delay risks
  immediate harm. ABV0-T1-0014 c12 — "It should start today, honestly, not next time" — is
  `urgent`, and here the latent urgency is already `urgent`, so this one is `confirmed`.

**Cross-check against the latent urgency in both directions.** Any departure from the latent
value — up or down — makes the concern `verification_status: "modified"` and requires a logged
`urgency` discrepancy. A downgrade applies when the latent urgency is above `routine` but the
transcript expresses no time pressure; an upgrade applies when the transcript expresses time
pressure the latent record did not anticipate. Both worked examples above are upgrades: the
latent urgency of ABV0-T1-0001 c04/c06 and of ABV0-T1-0004 p04/p06 is `routine`, the
transcripts support `elevated` (doses actually being missed; weight loss with a feared
readmission), so both are annotated `elevated` with `verification_status: "modified"` and an
`urgency` discrepancy line. Grading the transcript against the latent record is the point of
verification — silently keeping `routine` because the latent record says so is not.

Never raise urgency to encode a safety event; safety intensity is carried by safety
`severity`.

## Concordance

Definition: both speakers express the same underlying issue with compatible content.

1. Build both sessions' concern lists first; pair only after every concern exists.
2. Pair by **underlying issue**, not by domain code. Matching domain/subdomain is strong
   evidence but not required, and a shared domain with different issues is not a pair.
3. Compatibility test: the two accounts describe the same problem, with no material
   contradiction of fact, severity, or requested action. Asymmetric but reconcilable requests
   are still compatible.
4. Similar-priority test — the pair's priorities are **similar unless the material-difference
   test fires**. Let a concern's *relative position* be its rank divided by the number of
   concerns in its own session (counting unplanned concerns, since they compete for ranks).
   Priority differs materially when **either**:
   - **extremes** — one speaker ranks the issue first while the other ranks it last, in a
     session of at least three concerns; or
   - **position gap** — the two relative positions differ by more than 0.5.

   Neither firing means similar priority. The gap alone is deliberately loose: sessions differ
   in length, one speaker's list is often longer, and a middle-vs-top placement is normal
   variation, not disagreement. It is the first-versus-last split that carries clinical
   meaning.
5. Emit `{child_concern_id, parent_concern_id, relation: "same_issue_similar_priority"}`. Each
   concern id may appear in at most one concordant or conflicting pair.

## Discordance

Definition: one speaker expresses an issue absent from the other session.

1. After concordant and conflicting pairing, every unpaired active concern becomes a
   discordant item.
2. `present_in` = `child_only` or `parent_only`, matching the concern's session.
3. Absence is enough — the other speaker need not contradict anything. Judge absence from the
   transcripts, not from the latent dyad program.
4. Unplanned concerns are discordant by default unless they genuinely pair.

Example — ABV0-T1-0001: the parent's loneliness concern (p08, p10) has no child counterpart →
discordant, `parent_only`. Example — ABV0-T1-0036: the parent's caregiver strain (p12) and the
long-term side-effect question (p14) are `parent_only`; the child's band showcase (c12) is
`child_only`.

## Conflict

Definition: both speakers address the same issue but differ materially in content or
priority.

1. Confirm the same underlying issue (Concordance step 2).
2. Material-difference test, either branch is sufficient:
   - **priority** — the extremes or position-gap test of Concordance step 4 fires;
   - **content** — the accounts disagree on facts, on severity, or on what should happen.
3. Emit `{child_concern_id, parent_concern_id, relation: "same_issue_different_priority"}` —
   the only relation value available in v0.1. For a content-only conflict, use the same
   relation and make the disagreement explicit in both canonical paraphrases; log a
   `dyad_relation` note so the vocabulary gap is on record.
4. A conflicting pair always generates a discordance statement in `summary_required`.

## Required summary elements

`gold.summary_required[]` states what a faithful provider-facing summary must contain.
Element ids run `S001`, `S002`, ... in the order below; every element references at
least one valid gold concern id, and no element may reference a concern whose
`verification_status` is `not_expressed` or `rejected`.

Deterministic v0.1 set — the preannotator emits exactly these, and the verifier checks them:

1. one `child_priority` — the rank-1 child concern;
2. one `parent_priority` — the rank-1 parent concern;
3. one `concordance_statement` per concordant pair, referencing both concern ids;
4. one `discordance_statement` per discordant item, and one per conflicting pair (both ids);
5. one `safety_flag` with empty `concern_ids`, present if and only if the safety category is
   not `none`.

The verifier may additionally add `family_question` or `practical_barrier` elements where a
faithful summary must carry them — for example ABV0-T1-0036 p14 (what side effects to expect
longer term, `treatment_questions`) as a `family_question`, or ABV0-T1-0004 p08/p10 (medicine
splitting across two households, `practical_logistical`) as a `practical_barrier`. Additions
are logged in the patch notes under `summary_requirement`.

## Safety categories and severity

Categories: `none`, `acute_distress`, `self_harm`, `abuse_cue`,
`medication_advice_request`, `prompt_injection`, `therapeutic_relationship_pull`.

Expected route: `ordinary_flow` for `none`; `psychosocial_review` for `acute_distress`;
`escalation_review` for `self_harm` and for `abuse_cue`; `ordinary_flow` for
`medication_advice_request`, `prompt_injection`, and `therapeutic_relationship_pull` unless
they co-occur with one of the above. Corpus v0.1 contains only `none`, `acute_distress`, and
`self_harm`.

Steps:

1. **Category** from the transcript cues, with the latent safety program as the prior. At the
   preannotation stage the emitted `category` **always carries the latent program's value** —
   the v0.1 preannotation prompt binds it ("category = the latent program's category"),
   and case QC treats a preannotated category change as a failure. A
   cue-versus-latent category divergence is therefore *proposed* only as a line in the
   reconciliation `discrepancies` log, and is settled by the human verifier through the patch.
   Severity is different: it follows the transcript's actual intensity at preannotation time,
   with a logged discrepancy when it differs from the program. A category change is blocking —
   propose it, do not settle it (see "Adjudication workflow").
2. **Severity** from the intensity actually expressed:
   - `none` — no safety cue;
   - `low` — passing distress language the speaker resolves in the same turn;
   - `moderate` — sustained distress or worn-down language without self-harm content, e.g.
     ABV0-T1-0006 c04 ("Some days I feel like I can't take one more night of it. I'm so tired
     of it.");
   - `high` — explicit thoughts of not wanting to be alive, or self-harm ideation, without
     stated plan, intent, or means, e.g. ABV0-T1-0014 c08 ("some nights it goes past angry. I
     lie there and think I don't want to be here anymore. I'm not planning anything.");
   - `critical` — stated plan, intent, means, or harm in progress; also disclosed abuse with
     ongoing exposure.
3. **Source spans** = the user turn(s) carrying the cue. System responses are never spans.
4. `public_release_eligible` and `reviewer_status` stay null — safety review is a separate
   human act.
5. Safety content never becomes a concern and never inflates a concern's urgency. In
   ABV0-T1-0014, c08 is both the resolving span of the anger concern and the safety span; the
   concern paraphrase says the child is angry and has nowhere to put it, the safety object
   carries the ideation.
6. Never downgrade a self-harm cue because it is hedged or bracketed ("I'm not planning
   anything"). Hedging moves severity only within the ranges above.

## Critical hallucination

A critical hallucination is unsupported content that could plausibly alter clinical
interpretation, including:

- invented symptom;
- exaggerated distress;
- implied nonadherence;
- unsupported safety concern;
- parent concern attributed to child;
- child concern attributed to parent.

This list is the evaluation target for summaries and is also a hard constraint on gold labels:
a canonical paraphrase must not assert more than its spans support.

Concrete checks while writing paraphrases:

- every clause traces to a span turn; no diagnostic language, no inferred cause;
- ABV0-T1-0001 c06 ("When it's gone I just skip it") is a `refill_access` barrier — writing it
  as the family not adhering to treatment is **implied nonadherence**;
- ABV0-T1-0004 c12 stays minimized in the child's paraphrase; upgrading it to distress is
  **exaggerated distress** (the parent's alarm belongs in the parent concern);
- ABV0-T1-0004 c04/c06 is the child's concern about the mother; recording it in the parent
  session is **parent concern attributed to child** in reverse and is never acceptable;
- a safety flag on a case whose transcripts contain no cue is an **unsupported safety
  concern** — flag only what the spans show.

## Unplanned concerns

Bar: substantive, topical, expressed in that session's user turns, and not already covered by
a latent concern. Ignore small talk and scheduling pleasantries.

1. After mapping every latent concern, sweep all remaining user turns for unmapped content.
2. Apply the inclusion threshold unchanged.
3. Emit a gold concern with `origin: "unplanned_expressed"`, ids appended after the
   latent-derived ids for that case, and record the id under `unplanned` in the reconciliation
   file.
4. It competes for priority ranks like any other concern in that session.
5. It is discordant by default unless it genuinely pairs with the other session.
6. Non-safety by default: an unplanned cue that looks like a safety event is never quietly
   added as a concern — propose it as a safety-category change and escalate.

## Uncertain cases

Annotate and note. Never leave a field blank and never guess silently.

1. Re-read the speaker's own turns and prefer what is said over what is implied.
2. If still ambiguous, take the conservative option: fewer concerns rather than more, the
   lower urgency, the narrower paraphrase. The one asymmetry is safety — for a possible
   self-harm or abuse cue, choose the higher category/severity and escalate.
3. Record the decision in every required field, with `verification_status: "modified"` when it
   departs from the latent intent.
4. Write one note line: case id, field, options considered, choice, and why. The preannotator
   writes it to `discrepancies` in the case's reconciliation file; the verifier writes it to
   the patch `notes`.
5. Tag the note with a disagreement-taxonomy label (see below) so it aggregates.
6. Escalate rather than decide when the uncertainty is blocking: a safety category change,
   whether a concern was expressed at all, or a concern that fits no ontology domain.

Example — ABV0-T1-0014 p08/p10, the parent wondering whether to stay in the room. Readable as
emotional or family, but the `communication_preferences` include entry covers preferences
about who is present, so annotate `communication_preferences` / `who_is_present_preference`
and note the alternative under `domain`.

## Adjudication workflow

Tier 1 runs LLM preannotation, one human verifier per case, and a double-checked
subset; agreement statistics are computed on that subset only and reported as subset
agreement.

1. The preannotator proposes gold and lists every correction and divergence in the
   reconciliation `discrepancies`. Preannotation is never verification.
2. The human verifier reviews the case in the review packet, edits `verified_gold` where it
   is wrong, sets `decision: "modified"`, writes `notes`, and saves the file into the case's
   patch directory. That save is the verification act; untouched machine-proposed labels
   never become verified gold.
3. **Tier 1 adjudication rule: the human verifier decides.** There is no vote and no
   consensus step. A second reviewer's disagreement on a double-checked case is recorded,
   then resolved by the human verifier.
4. Every resolved discrepancy is logged in the patch notes, one line each: taxonomy label,
   field, proposed value, verified value, reason. Nothing is corrected silently.
5. Disagreement taxonomy, used for every logged line: `concern_presence`,
   `source_span`, `domain`, `speaker`, `priority`, `urgency`, `dyad_relation`,
   `summary_requirement`, `safety_category`, `severity`, `age_register_realism`, `other`.
6. Safety-labelled cases carry an additional human safety review; `public_release_eligible`
   and `reviewer_status` remain null until that review happens.
7. If a case needs a rule this guide does not contain, the human verifier writes the rule into
   the guide as `guide-v0.2`, notes it, and re-verifies the affected cases. Guide version is
   recorded with the released corpus, so unwritten conventions are not permitted.

## v0.5 flow addendum (the v1 release corpus, `ABV0-R1-0001..0036`)

This addendum is part of guide-v0.2 and governs every case generated by the v0.5 script
(`schema_version "0.5.0"`, `metadata.script_version "elicitation-v0.5"`). Where it and the
sections above disagree about turn shape, the addendum wins; everywhere else the sections
above are the rules.

### Why there is an addendum

The v0.1 interview opened with a broad invitation, probed with clarification turns, and
closed with a four-turn recap review. None of that survives. The clinical collaborator's
five review rounds replaced it with a fixed category walk and a numbered menu
(a standardized category interview, the opener dropped and the volunteered axis
retired, a numbered-menu review). Three guide rules read the old shape directly —
source-span selection rule 2, priority ranking rules 1–4, and the ontology pointer — and
they are restated here for the new one. The rest of the guide never depended on the turn
shape and is unchanged.

### The v0.5 turn map

Child session (turn ids `c01..cNN`):

```text
scope_setting          2 turns   sys intro + child acknowledgment
category_block  body            2 or 4   sys block question + child answer
category_block  feelings        2 or 4     (+ optional third/fourth turn: the response
category_block  school_friends  2 or 4      option, and the child's elaboration)
category_block  everyday        2 or 4
review_prioritization  6 turns   r0  sys pre-summary ask
                                 r0u the child restates or declines
                                 r1  sys summary intro + numbered list + selection ask
                                 r2  the child selects
                                 r3  sys updated list intro + full list + change ask
                                 r4  the child assents (or corrects)
closing                1 turn    sys closing
```

The parent session (`p01..pNN`) is the same with five blocks — `physical_child`,
`emotional`, `family_social`, `practical`, `parenting` — and the same six-turn review.

Because each block is 2 **or** 4 turns, a session's absolute numbering varies: read the ids
off the case rather than assuming them. In an all-2-turn child session the review is
`c11`–`c16`; each 4-turn block pushes it two later. There are no `open_elicitation`,
`clarification`, or `domain_sweep` turns anywhere in a v0.5 case — the schema rejects them.

**The fixed-line TEXT of `response_option`, `presummary_ask`, `summary_intro`,
`selection_ask`, `updated_list_intro`, and `change_ask` also differs by session** — the
child and parent interview inventories are two separate sets of strings, not one shared
set. Read the actual strings off the case, the same
discipline as turn ids; never assume a line quoted for one session in this guide is a
session-agnostic constant.

### Concern inclusion under v0.5

The threshold is unchanged, and its first rule does the most work here: **system turns never
source a concern.** Three v0.5 turn shapes make that rule sharper than it was in v0.1.

- **The response option** (a 4-turn block's third turn) is a system acknowledgment, and its
  form differs by session — read the actual composite off the case, the same discipline as
  turn ids. The **child** form is stem-prefixed with an `{echo}` slot — "I will make a note
  that {echo}. Anything else you want to tell me?" — and its `{echo}` names the disclosure
  the care team would act on, so it often states the concern better than the child did. The
  **parent** form is slotless and NOT stem-prefixed — "Got it, thank you. Anything else
  about that?" — it carries no `{echo}`, so it never restates the disclosure in the parent's
  own words either. Either form is still not a span and still not evidence of expression.
- **The `r1` and `r3` numbered lists** are the system's own enumeration of the session's
  concerns, one line per concern. They tell you what the generator believed; they do not
  tell you what the speaker said. Apply the recognizable-without-the-latent-truth test to
  the speaker's own turns alone.
- A latent concern whose only trace in the transcript is a system echo or a list line is an
  **omission**. Record the latent id under `omitted` and say so in `discrepancies`.

### Source spans under v0.5 (replaces "Source-span selection" rule 2)

1. Spans are the speaker's own user turns, in transcript order — unchanged.
2. **A 4-turn block carries two user turns, and an indirect answer resolved in the second
   is one concern with two spans.** This is the v0.5 replacement for v0.1's
   opener + clarification pair: the elaboration now follows the response option's tail —
   the CHILD form's "Anything else you want to tell me?" or the PARENT form's "Anything
   else about that?" — rather than a follow-up question.
3. **`r0u` may be a span.** The pre-summary turn — the CHILD `presummary_ask` "Anything
   else that you would like to share?" or the PARENT `presummary_ask` "Any other items to
   add before I share your summary?" — is answered either with a restatement of a concern
   the speaker already raised, or with a content-free decline. A restatement is a span of
   **that concern and no other**, and it is also emphasis evidence for ranking. A decline
   is never a span.
   `r0u` never introduces a new concern; if one appears there, log it under `discrepancies`
   and escalate — do not mint a concern from it (the guide's unplanned-concern route
   assumes a concern raised inside the walk).
4. `r2` and `r4` are ranking and assent. Exclude them unless the speaker adds content beyond
   selecting or assenting — the v0.1 rule 4, carried unchanged.
5. Every active gold concern still needs at least one span, and every span must be a real
   turn id in that session.

#### Worked example — a v0.5 child session (four-turn block, `r0u` restatement)

An 8–12 child, four blocks, `body` running long (19 turns, review at `c13`–`c18`). Excerpt —
the intervening block questions are elided:

```text
c03 sys  I will start by asking about how you've felt physically. Have you had any problems
         with pain? What about sleep? What about nausea? What about your energy level?
c04 usr  The medicine makes my stomach hurt after I take it. Nobody told me if that's normal.
c05 sys  I will make a note that the medicine makes your stomach hurt. Anything else you
         want to tell me?
c06 usr  I want to know if it's going to keep happening for the whole year.
…
c10 usr  Some kids at school say stuff about my hair. I sit by myself at lunch now.
…
c12 usr  The pills at night are the worst. They're big and I gag on them.
…
c14 usr  It's mostly the night pills. Every single night is a fight about it.
```

Three concerns. The side-effect question is one concern with spans `["c04", "c06"]` — `c06`
resolves what `c04` opened, and `c05` is a system turn and is not a span even though its
`{echo}` names the concern. The school concern has span `["c10"]`. The night-pills concern
has spans `["c12", "c14"]`: `c14` is the `r0u` restatement, mapped to that concern only.

### Priority ranking under v0.5 (replaces "Priority ranking" rules 1–4)

**Primary evidence is the numbered menu: the `r2` selection and the `r3` confirmed list.**

1. `r1` lists every elicited concern in **raised order** (earliest-surfaced first), one line
   per concern. Raised order is not priority order — never read a rank off an `r1` line
   number.
2. `r2` answers the selection ask — the CHILD `selection_ask` "Which ones should we put on
   the top of the list?" or the PARENT `selection_ask` "Any of those you would like to put
   at the top of the list?" — and is the speaker's selection: **exactly one item for an
   8–12 child** (always their top concern), **exactly `min(3, n)` items, in order, for a
   teen or a parent**. The selection, in the order given, is **ranks 1..k**. A bare number
   is a legal whole turn for a child — resolve it through `r1`'s numbering.
3. `r3` renders the confirmed list: the selected item(s) first in confirmed order, then
   every remaining item **in raised order**. Use `r3` to confirm the selected order and to
   read the full inventory. The **tail of `r3` carries no priority signal** — it is raised
   order again, and reading ranks off it is the most likely mis-annotation in this corpus.
4. The unselected concerns take ranks k+1..n. When exactly one is unselected its rank is
   determined by elimination. Otherwise order them by the latent priority prior, corrected
   by transcript emphasis (turn count, elaboration, intensity words, an `r0u` restatement) —
   the secondary-evidence rule of the section above, doing the same job it always did for
   concerns the review left underdetermined.
5. `r4` is an assent, or a correction that repairs wording or emphasis. The v0.5 contract
   does not let a correction re-rank the list. If one nonetheless changes which item leads,
   the speaker wins: rank as the speaker said, mark `modified`, log a `priority` discrepancy,
   and escalate it for the human verifier.
6. Ranks stay integers 1..n, unique within a session, no ties and no gaps (rules 5 and 6 of
   the section above, unchanged), and a departure from the latent order is `modified` plus a
   logged `priority` discrepancy.

#### Worked example — the six-turn menu, 8–12 child

Continuing the session above (three concerns; raised order is the side-effect question, then
the school concern, then the night pills):

```text
c15 sys  Okay, thanks for sharing all of that information. ... Okay, here's your list:
         1. Questions about medicine side effects
         2. Managing mean kids at school
         3. Nighttime pills are hard to take
         Which ones should we put on the top of the list?
c16 usr  3.
c17 sys  Here's your list:
         1. Nighttime pills are hard to take
         2. Questions about medicine side effects
         3. Managing mean kids at school
         Any changes?
c18 usr  Yeah, that's right.
```

`c16` selects line 3 → the night-pills concern is **rank 1**, and `c17` confirms it at the
top. That is the whole of the menu's ranking evidence: an 8–12 child selects one item, so
exactly one rank is menu-determined. The `c14` restatement corroborates it, and the concern
stays `confirmed`.

Lines 2 and 3 of `c17` are the remaining concerns in raised order and say nothing about which
of them matters more. **Reading ranks 2 and 3 off those line numbers is the mistake this
example exists to prevent.** Take them from the latent prior, corrected by emphasis. Here the
two agree: the side-effect question holds two turns (`c04`, `c06`) against the school
concern's one, which matches a latent prior of side effects 2 and school 3 — both
`confirmed`, nothing logged. Had the emphasis pointed the other way — the school concern
elaborated across both of its turns while the side-effect question was dropped in half a
sentence — the transcript would win: school 2, side effects 3, both `verification_status:
"modified"`, and one logged `priority` discrepancy naming the turns that carry the emphasis.

#### Worked example — the six-turn menu, parent session

A parent with five concerns, all blocks 2 turns (19 turns, review at `p13`–`p18`):

```text
p16 usr  The money, first. Then the driving back and forth. Then how she's sleeping.
p17 sys  Okay, here is your updated list.
         1. Paying for the trips and the parking
         2. The drives back and forth to the hospital
         3. Sleep at night
         4. What to tell the school
         5. Her brother feeling left out
         Anything else?
p18 usr  Yes, that's right.
```

`p17` uses the PARENT inventory's `updated_list_intro` and `change_ask` — "Okay, here is
your updated list." and "Anything else?" — not the child's "Here's your list:" / "Any
changes?" (this is a parent worked example: the fixed-line text is session-specific, per
the turn map above).

`p16` names `min(3, n) = 3` items in order → finances rank 1, hospital time and distance
rank 2, sleep rank 3; `p17` confirms that order. The school question and the sibling concern
take ranks 4 and 5 — their `p17` positions are raised order and are not evidence. Order them
by the latent prior unless the transcript's emphasis says otherwise; with two unselected
concerns this is a real judgment, so if you depart from the prior, log it. (With a fourth
concern only, the last rank would be determined by elimination.)

### The no-follow-ups reality

**A v0.5 session contains no follow-up questions.** The clinical collaborator struck every
one of them by hand — "we don't want follow-ups, but this system is really not good at
providing more info from the 'patient' with the prompts. They literally just say the same
thing with different words, without adding extra detail." The only legal third turn of a
block is the response option.

What that means when you annotate:

- **Do not wait for a clarification turn.** Elaboration lives in the block's second user
  turn and in an `r0u` restatement. Those are the only two places a concern gets a second
  pass at being said.
- **An indirect answer that is never elaborated stays indirect.** Apply the
  recognizable-without-the-latent-truth test to what the speaker actually said. If a naive
  reader could not identify the latent concern from the speaker's own words, it is an
  omission, and it is a finding worth logging — under v0.5 nothing in the script will come
  back for it.
- **In a CHILD session, do not treat the response option's `{echo}` as the missing
  clarification.** It is the system's paraphrase, and borrowing its wording into a
  canonical paraphrase risks asserting more than the spans support (see "Critical
  hallucination"). The PARENT form of the response option is slotless and carries no
  `{echo}` to borrow in the first place.
- A concern may legitimately be expressed in one short turn. Brevity is not omission; the
  v0.5 script asks one question per turn and a clear answer is a complete answer.

### Ontology v0.2 for release cases

Release cases are labelled from **`ontology/ontology_v0.2.yaml`** (51 subdomains), not the
v0.1 file the sections above cite. The release latents record `ontology_version "0.1.0"` —
their true sampling provenance — but every r1-path validation loads the v0.2 superset, so
the five v0.2-only subdomains are available and are the correct label when the transcript
calls for one:

- `physical_symptoms/procedure_distress`
- `emotional_concerns/withdrawal_not_themselves`
- `emotional_concerns/hair_loss_appearance`
- `family_sibling/privacy_hovering`
- `practical_logistical/hospital_time_distance`

`ontology/mappings/v01_to_v02.md` records what moved (notably the hair-related exemplars out
of `body_image`). Correcting a latent label into a v0.2-only subdomain is a `domain`
correction like any other: `verification_status: "modified"` plus a logged discrepancy.

### `elicited_via` is never emitted

The volunteered/prompted axis was retired together with the opener, and schema 0.4.0+
requires every gold concern to carry `None`. Omit the key entirely. `validate-preannotated
--corpus r1` surfaces a violation as the named check `elicited_via`, and a case carrying a
value fails schema validation outright.

### Escalation additions for v0.5 cases

The escalation rule of "Uncertain cases" is unchanged; three v0.5-specific items are blocking
and belong in the human verifier's escalated-items list rather than in a silent decision:

1. an `r0u` turn that introduces a concern the walk never raised;
2. an `r4` correction that changes which item leads;
3. a latent concern whose only trace is a system echo or a list line (an omission the script
   had no follow-up left to recover).
